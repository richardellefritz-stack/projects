**Status:** GO 2026-08-26

**Amends:** Provenance model practice for *what* may load-bear. Does not repeal provenance-gate, CONFIRM, or the Deploy Handoff Rule.

---

## Why this exists

Paywalled monographs (Guttmann 2004, Young 1984, Kyle 1998, Fagan 2011, and others) cannot be opened on the operator's time or budget. Requiring page-level verification of those books before a page ships made the ledger unusable as a production path.

The gate stays. The allowed *support* changes.

---

## Rule

1. **Open URL only.** A publishable page may load-bear only on sources the operator can open today for free, in full, at a stable URL:
   - public-domain texts (Wikisource, Perseus, Latin Library, LacusCurtius, out-of-copyright Loeb)
   - official documents and tables (World Athletics, historical FA/IFAB laws, museum/collection records)
   - open-access articles and books with page or section locators (including LA84 Digital Library PDFs)

2. **Face of the document.** A claim may not exceed what its cited document shows on its face. A locator that requires extra inference to support the claim is not a locator. Narrow the claim.

3. **Documentary vs interpretive.**
   - Documentary claims: open document + locator.
   - Interpretive claims: name the support in the prose ("historians of sport generally treat…") **or** cite an open article/PDF with pages. "Sport historians have shown X" with no open source is blocked.

4. **Paywalled monographs** remain in the corpus as leads, further reading, or pending claims. They never block a page and never carry a factual claim alone.

5. **Transcriptions.** Wikisource and similar hosts are transcriptions. Cite the underlying work (e.g. FA Laws 1863) and treat the host as the access copy, not the authority.
