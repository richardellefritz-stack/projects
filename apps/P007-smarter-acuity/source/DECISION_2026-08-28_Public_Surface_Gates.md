## 4. The five gates

### G1 · Relevant
A living reader can see why this is worth a minute. Current fight, last 5–50 years, or older history they can still use. Fail is fog. Fail is not “too old.”

### G2 · Informative
One reusable number, document, list, or mechanism.

### G3 · Helpful, inspiring, or a defense shown in the object
Not chanted in the caption.

### G4 · Shareable
Line 1 is the fact or the question. Grade 8–10 English. One keepable object. 40 or 80 words.

### G5 · Source discipline without smothering
Do not invent. Captions cite nothing. One good public source may carry a paragraph of meaning. Fail: refusing a contemporary fight because the name is not yet in `/corpus/`.

### Amendment B · Published fights, quotation vs scene

Worked case: *Colin in Black & White* (Netflix, 2021). Inside Edition, 2 Nov 2021: “The former NFL player compares the league’s draft process to the slave auction block.” Head Case was right to verify. The stall (“exact slogan must already be in `/corpus/`”) was wrong.

1. A public face is enough to sit the fight. Does not have to be in `/corpus/` first. A ten-second news find is a legal door.
2. Three layers: spoken line / outlet caption or headline / image or staging. Do not collapse them. Putting “slave auction” in Kaepernick’s mouth as a quotation invents a sentence.
3. Verify with Screenwriter. Do not veto the topic.
4. Defense is the distinction, not the dunk. The West ended the Atlantic slave trade it had practiced. A published, voluntary, measured contest ending in a contract is not chattel. Do not write “own the libs.”
5. Still forbidden: invented quotes; treating a headline as a spoken sentence; posting; `/corpus/` writes without a GO. SmAq writes after the keepable card exists, not before.
