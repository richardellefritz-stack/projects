# A Tool for Gathering a City

A city does not gather itself. People have to be brought into the same air, at the same hour, for a reason they did not invent that morning.

Vitruvius, *De Architectura* 1.3.1, classes among public works the places arranged for common use: harbors, fora, porticoes, baths, theatres, and walks. The page is a list of methods for gathering a public, not a named material and not a crane.

[CLM-TT-0001]

You can feel a gathering-tool fail without a manual. The line did not move. The voice did not carry. The exit was a rumor. That notice is not on Vitruvius's list. It stays a notice.

I used to tell classes that a syllabus is a tool for gathering twenty people who would not otherwise have met. They groaned, and then they noticed the room had already done the same work before I spoke.

## Handing On a Method of Building

A tool that cannot be handed on dies with the person who first used it. Teaching is how a method outlives a morning.

Vitruvius writes, in the Book I preface and in 1.1, that he has set out the principles of the discipline so they may be known. That is the face used here: a method written down for later hands. It is not a modern syllabus and not a stolen treatise from later in the week.

[CLM-TT-0002]

If we cannot say how a method is taught, we are only admiring a result. Admiration is reading. Teaching is the check.
